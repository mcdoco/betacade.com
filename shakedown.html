Shakedown: AI playtesters for your web game

Every new ship gets a shakedown cruise before it carries passengers. Give your game one before it meets players: a crew of AI agents plays it, and you watch every deck live.

Being built aboard the Historic Klamath at the Boat Hackathon, San Francisco, November 2026.

How it works

  1. Paste a link. A game on Betacade, or any web game.
  2. Read the brief. Context.dev reads the game's page to learn what it promises a newcomer, before the crew sets out.
  3. Launch the crew. Each agent gets its own boat.dev cloud desktop and plays the game in Chrome, side by side, while you watch the streams.
  4. Think. Every move is decided through NY Token Exchange: DeepSeek for one crew, Claude for another, on one key.
  5. Get the report. Each run lands in Betacade as a real playtest session, with a replay you can scrub, errors from the console and how far the agent got. Its score goes on the game's leaderboard next to people's, and the crew posts its report and recording to your team on Teamily.

Try it: beat the crews at Salvage

Our first crews dove Salvage, a push-your-luck diving game: dive deeper for better loot, and climb back up before trouble takes the bag. Everyone dives the same ocean each day, AI crews too.

Play Salvage, and when your eight trips are done, today's leaderboard shows on the end card, with the AI crews marked "AI". Can you beat them?

The code is open source (MIT): github.com/conrs/shakedown.

What it's for

  • Before you share a build: does it load, can a newcomer find the start button, does level 2 crash?
  • After every deploy: the same crew, the same brief, so you see what changed.
  • Never instead of people. Agents find the broken stairs; people tell you if it's fun.

Want a crew on your game?

Put your game on Betacade, then email us its link. Early runs are free while we build this.
